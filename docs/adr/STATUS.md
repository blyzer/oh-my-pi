A daemon reporting another policy (`EnvdError::DaemonPolicyMismatch`, naming both policies) or none (`DaemonPolicyUnreported`) is refused: on the owner connection the session runs an embedded environment under its own policy with that refusal in its fallback notice, and on the session connection the attach fails. A session whose policy comes from settings made in its own process (`--add-dir`, a cfg script, an agent class) spawns no daemon, since one would resolve the configured policy (`AttachOptions::spawn_policy`, `DaemonPolicyNotSpawnable`); a spawned daemon that still reports another policy is stopped, and spawns forward the session's profile. A child resumed with `--resume` presents its journaled class before its environment starts, and a composition whose session presents another policy than its environment enforces is refused (`HeadlessError::EnvironmentPolicyDrift`). Open: a chat that switches to a child of another policy from inside (`/resume`) keeps its environment's policy; `ConJournal::resync` now says so once on the console. 2026-10-08 (disabled-network diag): a disabled network has no broker to record a refused fetch and a quiet client (`curl -s`) prints no marker, and no backend records a connection its kernel refuses, so the in-process shell hands the spawn wrapper each external launch it composes, with its program and expanded arguments (`SpawnWrapper::observe_launch`) and a session attempt with no network records whether one argument is a network URL (the whole argument `scheme://host…`, any scheme but `file`) handed to a program that can run. A failed command with that fact and no refusal gets the mode's locator text (`disabled`, or the broker that could not start) from the marker's once-per-session slot; a marker outranks it, `scoped` is unchanged. Accepted costs: a program given a URL that fails for another reason spends the slot on a hedged diag; URLs inside longer arguments, read from files or stdin, raw addresses, and the children of `xargs`, `find -exec`, `sort --compress-program` and `ifne` leave no sign; a pipeline whose final stage succeeds after a quiet client failed (`curl -s URL | head`) ends `Succeeded` and reports nothing, which is the observed symptom's most common shape; under `scoped` a quiet client that bypasses the proxy still fails with no sign; a refuse-all broker under `disabled` would give a real one and is left to the owner.. 2026-10-08 (read fetch): `read@3` is judged per call by one classification its executor also dispatches on (`classify_target`), over every spelling its path may split into: a URL is an anonymous fetch while `tools.fetch.enabled` allows it, an internal URI fetches as its resolver reports (`ssh://`, `issue://` and `pr://` with credentials, `mcp://` by its server's mount: remote always, local only at a tier reaching the network), and everything else is a document read; its fetching targets are its locators, so `always-ask` asks once per host per session. The maximum always holds a credentialed fetch in production (`ReadPolicy::credentialed_fetch`), and a call beyond it is refused. Language servers, debug adapters, the Obsidian CLI and local MCP servers at the `read` or `write` tier are environment-ambient, governed by their configuration, never `exec` in `read`'s maximum. The environment's instrumenting wrapper now forwards argument-scoped effects and fetch locators, which no production registration (`computer` included) received before. 2026-10-09 (attached posture notice): the session's one `approval-posture` notice was posted only by the kernel's own admission, which over an attached daemon admits no environment tool, so such a session never reported its posture; `install_tool_authority` now gives the kernel's admission and the environment tool executor one shared report, which the executor posts before the environment admits a call, so the first admission of any tool reports it once, carrying the sandbox state the session probes (the state its policy-matched daemon enforces); a switch to another session re-arms it, a rewind does not. Typed notices stay out of the model's projection. 2026-10-09 (search fetch): `grep`, `glob` and `ast_grep` are judged per call by the root kinds their executors route by: a `grep` URL root is an anonymous fetch and its other URI roots fetch as the resolver `read` uses reports, a `glob` walk over `ssh://` is a credentialed fetch, and an `ast_grep` URL root an anonymous one; `grep` and `ast_grep` refuse URL roots while `tools.fetch.enabled` is off. A `dyn` device call (`ast_grep` is a device under the environment's tools policy) is judged by its arguments and names its hosts, instead of taking its declared maximum.
